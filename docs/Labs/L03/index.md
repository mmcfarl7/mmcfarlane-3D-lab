# L3 Designing something small

## Design
My initial brainstorm was to make something as simple as possible since my last print was too detailed and thus caused a rather lengthy printing time. I settled on a rectangular print of a little desktop knickknack that I figured my other sister would enjoy since it made me think of her. The design is simple, goofy, and it didn't require too much time to design, being just a simple 1.5 in by 1.5 in square with a .35 in extrusion with half the height being a depth extrusion for the face and eyes and tongue of the smiley face.

## Research
Infill pattern 1: Grid/Rectilinear. A simple crosshatch pattern that is not only simple in appearance but simple in explanation. Gives consistent strength in all directions and perfect for all-purpose prints.
Infill patter 2: Gyroid. A continuous wavy structure. It offers excellent isotropic strength and distributes strength evenly along a part. Ideal for more lightweight but sturdy components under multi-directional stress.
Infill pattern 3: Concentric. Follows the shape of the part and provides extra support near the walls. Used for aesthetic or reinforcement along the walls.
To answer the prompt of why infill percent matters, it is very straightforward. The more infill, the more solid a part and the stronger it becomes at the cost of more print time. The less infill, the less strength and the less time it takes to print. As you can see above, different prints affect different types of stresses and forces, each one being better than others for certain types of force or stress.
## Preprocessor and Printing

As you can see, I chose the most basic and simple shapes with the simplest face for the easiest possible printing job. No need for scale since it was exactly on the parameter guidelines of 1.5 inches diameter. I used 20% infill instead of the default 15% just to make it a bit more solid. I also used triangular infill patterns instead of the default since it reduced the print time from 20 to 15 minutes. I thickened the wall by 1 mm from 2 to 3 mm since I wanted to ensure the walls of the little box shaped model were thick but not too much, just a slight little nudge of extra strength. Wall thickness itself can be used at different thicknesses for bearing different loads and repeling different forces.
