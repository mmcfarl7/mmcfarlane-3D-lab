# L09 - Laser Cutting

## Orientation/Beginning

This was the first time I had ever needed to use something so complicated and dangerous. This project began by all the students in the lab going to the Super Fab Lab where we were given basic instructions and safety guardrails to how to work and operate a laser cutter under trained supervision. The task was simple: Make at least 8 pieces, 3 being unique shapes, and assemble them to create something, anything, as long as it fits together using the laser cutter. At first, I approached the teacher asking for advice but he just smiled and shook his head and showed us some very simple and basic types of ideas, which I wasn't that interested in. I wanted to make something unique and functional but not overly complicated or too simple. I decided to seek advice elsewhere, so while I was on a call with my mother I asked her for any ideas of things made with wood fittings. She shrugged and randomly said "chair", and thus my idea was formed.

The design was ready, but before we continue, I'd like to mention a side note that was important to this lesson. I was introduced to a concept known as "Kerf", which is the clearance between the laser cutter's design to be cut vs the actual finished cut shape. For example, if I designed the shapes with a 1 mm border instead of the default line border of 0.265 mm border then the kerf would be roughly 0.5 mm into the border, the middle path of the border. While my shapes all ranged in sizes from the chair being 30 mm and the table being almost 60 mm, I found great difficulty in making exact measurements because of all the degrees of freedom Inkscape had compared to something like Creo where you put a number into the slot and that number stays that number until it changed by you. Thankfully, the supervisors in the super fab lab instructed us that at the default the kerf was so small it was near negligible, but just to be sure, I calculated the kerf and I estimated the range of the kerf to be somewhere around 0.1825 mm to 0.265 mm as per the path of the laser cutter.

## Laser Cutting

https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/0de9a1bd-4eb5-4c70-9dfb-4e6b4c922aca

Now, if you watched the video, allow me to tell you the safety steps BEFORE you hit the big red button... First: vents and ducts open to suck the dangerous fumes and bad smell away. Second: compressor and fan ON. Third: make sure everything in its place securely and that the laser is focused and on the RIGHT MATERIAL. As it would turn out what happened to cause all that smoke in the video was that step 1 was NOT followed even though I swore to God I did that because it was the first thing I did. But as you saw in the video, I realized what happened. You see, what happened before the cutting was the issue with setting up the printer to my computer. While I was conversing with the lead supervisor at the time, another employee was attempting to shoot a safety video (ironically) on the laser cutter, in doing so he undid step 1 and part of step 2 that I had done beforehand, so he closed the vents and turned off the compressor. Despite the abundance of white smoke, it was fine, no fire was started, but it smelled like a hot campfire for a minute I'll tell you that much. Nobody got hurt and I'm thankful everything went fine but this is why it's very important that even if you step away for just a minute you should always check that everything on the safety checklist is marked off BEFORE hitting the big red button.
