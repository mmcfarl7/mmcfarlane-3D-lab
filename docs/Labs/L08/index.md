# L08 - Design and print something useful

## Intro Paragraph - Smart Questions
Using the website https://www.toptal.com/designers/product-design/design-thinking-great-questions we were tasked with writing a paragraph about "Smart Questions" and why they matter. To me, the answer is more about micro or macro management to lead a design. Macro questions are Smart questions that are simple but open ended, leading you to think critically about the results and properties of a design that can lead to such a simple question being incredibly complicated like "Why did we pick this?" or "What will happen if...?" and so on and so forth. Micro questions are the refining Smart questions that polish the design like you're polishing a gemstone. If a flaw is discovered in this stage, it can reveal if the gemstone, or in this case a design, is effective, correct, or impure. These forms of Smart questions can help prevent unnecessary setbacks due to fear of asking "wrong questions" and protect the thought of asking questions as interruptions towards progress.

## Problem Statement

- The purpose of my 2 designs is to prevent water condensation from leaking onto wooden surfaces and the other design is to act as a gift and storage.
- The problem was mentioned about 3 weeks before the project began when my mother stated how she wanted to get a small jewelry box for my younger sister and gave her own box to make her happy, leaving her box-less.
- I designed this product for my mother in mind, but any general population wouldn't mind the object.
- My approach to the design was to make something obviously intended as a gift while also being functional.
## Design
<img width="1160" height="216" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/e31bee8d-5bd3-4e6d-be36-6c255c5c5d43" />

While I do not have sketches, I do have the design process in Creo. I took a 6 by 6-inch box (later changed to 4 by 4) and added two half circles of equal diameter to the top and left sides of the square. I then extruded the result up 4 inches. I then shelled the inside to have a 0.3-inch wall and created the same shape but extruding only 1 inch in height before adding a second extrusion via an offset of 0.3-0.4 inches (a safety allowance to prevent wiggle room or no sliding fit). I then copied and pasted the storage box's shape and extruded it upward 1 inch for two congruent shapes to make 2 coasters as I remember my mother also stating how she always feels like she doens't have enough. All this work was done on the "Top" plane view.

## Printing and Video
For the final printing, I realized an error of making the parts 6 by 6-inches which was too much for the printer to handle, even so it would've been HOURS of printing, which I didn't approve of. So, I edited my design to be only 4 by 4 inches via the "Scale" tool in Prusa slicer. I decided to print all 4 objects, the box and the lid and the 2 coasters, into 2 separate printers to save time. One thing I would've changed if I could've gone back is changed the print pattern and infill on the coasters from "Lightning" (chosen for speed) to "Gyroid" which would've been more supportive and maybe bump up the percentage of the infill from 10 to 30 percent. These things wouldn't have mattered for the box since it doesn't need to support a full glass of a drink, which will put a big load on the coaster and the infill supports inside. ## Lessons Learned
In the end, I was quite happy with my results and the reaction from my mother was joyous and flattered. I was more than proud of the final results, but I do wish that if I had a second chance, I would've changed the parameters of the infill for the coasters to be stronger, but upon testing they did seem to function without any visible or audible strain. Further refinements are likely.
